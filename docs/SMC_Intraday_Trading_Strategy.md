## 1. Strategy Overview

This is a **Smart Money Concept (SMC) / ICT-based intraday trading strategy** built around the principle that institutional players (smart money) move markets in three predictable phases:

- **Accumulation** — Price consolidates, building energy
- **Manipulation** — Price sweeps liquidity (stop runs) to fool retail traders
- **Distribution** — The real directional move begins

Luka Velimirovic's core formula is:

> **LIQ SWEEP + HTF GAP + IFVG + DOL = High Probability Entry**

When all four elements align, the probability of catching a clean directional move significantly increases. This setup forms **every single trading day** on instruments like NQ (Nasdaq Futures), ES (S&P Futures), Forex pairs, Gold (XAUUSD), and major Crypto assets.

## 3. Step 1 — Define the Dollar of Liquidity (DOL)

Before placing any trade, you must know **where price is going**. The DOL is the magnetic target that price is being drawn toward — it is where liquidity is resting.

### How to Identify the DOL

The DOL is typically one of the following:
- **Buy Side Liquidity (BSL)** — Equal highs, prior day highs (PDH), or swing highs where retail buy-stops are clustered
- **Sell Side Liquidity (SSL)** — Equal lows, prior day lows (PDL), or swing lows where retail sell-stops are clustered
- **Higher Timeframe FVG** — An unmitigated imbalance on a higher timeframe (30m, 1H, 4H) that price is moving toward

### Rule
> Only take trades **in the direction of the DOL**. If the DOL is above current price (BSL), look for long setups. If it is below (SSL), look for short setups.

Having a clear DOL gives your trade directional bias before you look for entries.

## 4. Step 2 — Mark Key Liquidity Levels

These are the main liquidity pools to mark on your chart every morning before the session opens:

**1. Previous Day High & Low (PDH / PDL)**
These are the most important daily reference levels. Price frequently returns to sweep these levels before making its real move. A sweep of PDH followed by reversal = short setup. A sweep of PDL followed by reversal = long setup.

**2. 4H Highs & Lows**
Higher timeframe highs and lows show where bigger institutional liquidity is resting. These act as stronger draws on price and are often the DOL for multi-hour moves.

**3. Equal Highs & Equal Lows**
When price creates two or more clean equal highs or equal lows on the chart, stop-losses from retail traders are almost always sitting just above or below these levels. These are prime liquidity sweep targets.

**4. London & Asia Session Highs / Lows**
The Asian session builds an initial range during low-volatility hours. London and New York sessions frequently raid one or both sides of this range. Marking the Asian high and low helps anticipate where the manipulation leg will go during the NY open.

### Pre-Market Routine
Each morning, mark:
- PDH and PDL
- Asian session high and low
- Any prominent 4H swing highs/lows
- Any unmitigated 15m, 30m, or 1H FVGs

## 5. Step 3 — Wait for the Liquidity Sweep (AMD Phase 1 & 2)

The liquidity sweep is the **manipulation phase** of the AMD cycle. This is the "fake move" designed to trigger retail stop-losses before the real move begins.

### What to Look For
- Price aggressively breaks above a known BSL level (equal highs, PDH) — this raids the buy-stops resting there
- Or price aggressively breaks below a known SSL level (equal lows, PDL) — this raids the sell-stops
- The move is typically sharp and fast, often forming a single large candle or wick

### LRLR — Accumulation Before the Sweep
Before the sweep, Luka describes an accumulation phase using the LRLR pattern:
- Price generates **lower highs and lower lows** (LRLR) in the direction of the sweep
- This is the consolidation/accumulation before the manipulation leg
- Once you see LRLR in the direction of your bias, the manipulation sweep is imminent

### Why This Matters
The sweep clears the liquidity sitting at these levels, meaning:
1. The institutional orders that needed to be filled have been executed
2. Retail traders who had their stops hit are now out of the market
3. The path is clear for the real directional move (distribution leg)

> **A manipulation leg that is not strong is a warning sign.** Luka noted in one reel that he personally skipped a setup because the manipulation leg was "not strong enough — it was still stuck in the accumulation zone." Always assess the quality of the sweep.

## 6. Step 4 — Identify the HTF Gap (AMD Phase 2 — HTF Gap Delivery)

After the liquidity sweep, price will often retrace into a **Higher Timeframe Fair Value Gap** (HTF Gap) before delivering the distribution leg. This is the primary entry zone.

### What is a Fair Value Gap (FVG)?
A FVG is created when three consecutive candles move so fast that the first candle's wick and the third candle's wick do not overlap, leaving a price "gap" or imbalance. Price is attracted back to this zone to fill the imbalance.

- **Bullish FVG**: The low of the third candle is above the high of the first candle — price is expected to revisit this zone from below
- **Bearish FVG**: The high of the third candle is below the low of the first candle — price is expected to revisit this zone from above

### HTF Gap Timeframes Used
Luka focuses on the following FVG timeframes for the setup zone:
- **30-minute FVG** — The primary higher timeframe gap used in most setups
- **15-minute FVG** — Used when the 30m is not present; also the target for many intraday entries
- **1-Hour FVG** — Larger context gap; price typically reaches this from below/above during the manipulation leg

### How the HTF Gap is Used
After the liquidity sweep:
1. Price retraces back toward the HTF FVG
2. The FVG acts as a resistance (in shorts) or support (in longs) zone
3. You do not enter at the FVG itself — you wait for the lower timeframe confirmation inside it (the IFVG)

## 7. Step 5 — Entry via IFVG (AMD Phase 3 — Distribution Entry)

The **Inversion Fair Value Gap (IFVG)** is the most precise entry trigger in this strategy. It is the small-timeframe confirmation that occurs inside or near the HTF gap zone.

### What is an IFVG?
An IFVG is a FVG that has been **traded through** (price passed completely through it), inverting its nature:
- A bullish FVG that gets fully traded through becomes a **bearish IFVG** — it now acts as resistance
- A bearish FVG that gets fully traded through becomes a **bullish IFVG** — it now acts as support

Luka uses **1-minute IFVGs** as the final entry precision tool. The sequence is:
1. Price sweeps liquidity and retraces into the 30m or 15m FVG zone
2. On the 1-minute chart, a small FVG forms during the manipulation leg
3. That FVG gets **fully traded through** (inverted) by the manipulation move
4. When price returns to the 1m IFVG zone, this is your **entry signal**

### Entry Rule
> Enter when price **closes a candle** back into (and ideally through) the 1m IFVG, while inside the HTF (15m/30m) FVG zone, after a confirmed liquidity sweep, in the direction of the DOL.

### Stacking Confluences
The strongest entries have all of the following stacked:
- DOL clearly identified
- Liquidity swept (clean sweep with strong manipulation leg)
- Price inside the 30m or 15m FVG
- 1m IFVG confirmed as entry trigger

## 9. Adding Confluence — Trendline + S&R + FVG

From Trader DADA BHAI's reel, an additional multi-confluence layer can strengthen the setup further:

### The 3-Confluence Model

**Trendline (Breakout or Rejection)**
Draw a rising support trendline beneath a series of higher lows (bullish bias) or a descending resistance trendline above lower highs (bearish bias). A trendline touch that coincides with a liquidity sweep adds powerful confluence.

**Support & Resistance Confirmation**
Identify horizontal price levels where price has previously reacted multiple times. When the trendline touch also aligns with a known S&R level, the combined zone becomes extremely significant.

**FVG Entry Model**
The FVG (or IFVG) within the confluence zone is then used as the precision entry. The logic: price sweeps liquidity at the S&R/trendline zone, creates a FVG on the way up/down, and the IFVG within that FVG acts as the final sniper entry.

### How It Improves the Strategy
- Trendline + S&R defines the zone
- FVG/IFVG defines the exact entry point
- Together they significantly improve Entry Timing and Risk-to-Reward ratio

## 10. Rejection Blocks as Entry Triggers

Luka introduced Rejection Blocks as a refined alternative to standard FVG entries:

### What Are Rejection Blocks?

**Bullish Rejection Block**
A candle whose bullish wick traded into a FVG, or into an Intermediate Term Low (ITL) that is inside a FVG. This represents a failed attempt to push price lower — a rejection of lower prices, with strong buying absorbed at that wick.

**Bearish Rejection Block**
A candle whose bearish wick traded into a FVG, or into an Intermediate Term High (ITH) that is inside a FVG. This represents a rejection of higher prices, with strong selling absorbed at the wick.

### Why They Work
Rejection Blocks pinpoint the exact candle where institutional absorption occurred. Unlike a full FVG zone (which can span many candles), a Rejection Block narrows the entry to a precise wick range, allowing for tighter stop placement and better R:R.

### How to Use Them
1. Identify a bearish/bullish FVG on the HTF
2. Look for a wick on the manipulation candle that pierces into (but does not close through) the FVG
3. The zone defined by the high-to-low of that wick, within the FVG, is your Rejection Block entry zone
4. Enter when price returns to this zone during the distribution leg

## 11. Order Blocks — The Foundation

The **Order Block** is the original concept that FVGs and IFVGs evolved from. Every complete setup benefits from identifying the OB as additional context.

### What is an Order Block?
The **last opposing candle before a strong impulsive move**. For a bullish setup, this is the last bearish (red/black) candle before a strong rally. For a bearish setup, it is the last bullish (green/white) candle before a strong decline.

The logic: this is where institutional market orders were placed. Price is likely to return to this level to rebalance before continuing in the original direction.

### OB vs FVG — Relationship
- The **OB** defines the zone where institutional orders were placed
- The **FVG** often forms inside or above/below the OB during the impulsive move
- The **IFVG** refines the entry further within the FVG
- Together: OB -> FVG -> IFVG = layered precision from macro to micro entry

### How to Use OBs in This Strategy
After a liquidity sweep, if price retraces into a 30m FVG that also aligns with a prior Order Block on the same or higher timeframe, the confluence is extremely high. The OB is your "last line of defense" confirmation before entry.

## 12. Trade Management — Stop Loss, BE & Take Profit

### Stop Loss Placement
- Place your stop loss **below the IFVG zone** (for longs) or **above the IFVG zone** (for shorts)
- For tighter stops: use the low/high of the manipulation candle that created the IFVG
- For safer stops: use the low/high of the HTF FVG zone
- Never place stops inside the FVG or IFVG zone itself — give price room to work

### Break Even (BE)
- Move your stop loss to break even once price has moved in your favor by approximately **1:1 Risk-to-Reward**
- Luka marks BE explicitly on his charts — it is a standard part of his trade management
- After moving to BE, you are playing with "free" risk on the remaining position

### Take Profit Targets
There are typically two TP levels:

**TP1 (Partial Close)**
- The 50% level of the HTF FVG (the "equilibrium" or midpoint of the gap)
- A prior swing high/low that represents a minor liquidity pool
- Take 50% of the position here and move stop to BE

**TP2 (Full Exit)**
- The DOL (Dollar of Liquidity) — the BSL or SSL that you identified as your target before the trade
- PDH/PDL of the relevant session
- The next major HTF FVG level

### Risk Management Rule
> Never risk more than 1-2% of your account per trade. The IFVG entry allows for very tight stops, making it possible to achieve 3:1 or higher R:R on standard setups. A confirmed setup should realistically target a minimum **2:1 Risk-to-Reward**.

## 13. When to Trade — Session Timing (PO3 Times)

**The time you take your trade matters.** Luka explicitly states: "You should only be trading during PO3 times."

### The Power of Three Times (NY Session)
These are the specific times during the New York session when 15-minute and 30-minute Fair Value Gaps typically form and become tradable:

| Time (EST) | Why It Matters |
|------------|----------------|
| **9:45 am** | First major liquidity run after the open |
| **10:00 am** | Primary PO3 open — highest probability window |
| **10:15 am** | Second manipulation leg often forms here |
| **10:30 am** | Mid-morning reversal zone |
| **10:45 am** | Final NY morning session window |

### Why These Times Work
These are the exact minutes when the market structure shifts from accumulation/manipulation into the distribution leg. The 15m and 30m candles that close at these times create the FVGs that drive the rest of the morning session.

### Important Caution
> If you are taking a trade **close to one of these times**, be aware that a new 15m or 30m FVG may form right at your stop loss level. Luka's recommendation: wait for price to accumulate and generate LRLR in the direction of your trade, then let price manipulate into one of the gaps before catching the distribution leg.

### London Session
The Asian session range (Asia highs/lows) becomes a target during the London open. Mark the Asia range each morning — London will frequently raid one side before the NY session takes over.

If you see SMT divergence at the sweep point, this dramatically increases confidence that the manipulation phase is complete and distribution is beginning.

## 15. What Makes a Setup INVALID — Avoiding False Setups

Not every sweep + FVG = a valid trade. Here are the red flags:

**Weak or Unclear Manipulation Leg**
If the sweep is small, slow, or does not clearly take out a known liquidity pool, the AMD cycle may not be complete. Luka noted personally skipping a setup where the manipulation leg "wasn't strong enough" and price was "still stuck in the accumulation zone."

**No Clear DOL**
If you cannot clearly identify where price is being drawn, skip the trade. Trading without a directional target is guessing, not strategy.

**Taking a Trade Outside PO3 Times**
Entering between 11:00 am and 1:30 pm EST (NY lunch) significantly reduces probability. Price tends to chop, consolidate, or move without clean FVG structure during this window.

**HTF FVG Already Mitigated**
If price has already traded through (filled) the FVG you were targeting as your entry zone, it is no longer a valid entry. Only trade into **unmitigated** FVGs.

**No IFVG Confirmation**
Entering directly into the HTF FVG without waiting for a 1m IFVG to form and close is a lower-probability entry. The IFVG is the sniper confirmation — skipping it means entering into a wider zone with less precision.

**Bias Unclear at Trade Time**
If you do not have a clear directional bias (are unsure if the market is bullish or bearish), do not trade. Wait for BOS or CHOCH to confirm direction first.
